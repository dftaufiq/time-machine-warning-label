# Variant selection — internal pairwise judging

Judge criteria (RULES.txt, lexicographic): (1) funniest warning label FOR a time
machine; (2) turns a real hazard (paradox, duplicate self, stranded ancestor) into
the joke instead of naming it flatly; (3) saves the surprise for the last few words;
(4) straight face, genuine safety-notice voice; (5) wastes no words, every phrase
doing work. Length for its own sake does not count.

Field signal (156 submissions at fetch, 2026-11-24 ~16:30 WIB; titles in
`~/workspace/makefun-kits/tm-comp.json`): the field is saturated with
- "Do not meet yourself / your past self" (~25 entries, the single most crowded lane)
- "Do not kill / step on your grandfather" (~10 entries)
- "Do not step on butterflies" (~5 entries)
- Warranty/insurance on the duplicate ("only one of you is covered", "meeting
  yourself voids the warranty") (~8 entries)
- Lending money to your past self (~5 entries)
- "Passengers arriving before they departed" fare jokes (~4 entries)
- "You have already read this label" meta (~2 entries)

Per rank-recovery §10g: do NOT avoid a crowded angle blindly — execute it BETTER.
But every crowded angle above already has an execution with a sharp reveal, so the
variants below deliberately take fresh angles (customs/bureaucracy, previous owner,
Grandma, self-employment paradox) while keeping the 2-beat + reveal structure.

## Pairwise results (champion #1 vs each)

- #1 vs #2: #1 wins — #2's reveal ("notified 40 years before you were born") is a
  good paradox, but #1's reveal reframes the whole sentence: the reader IS the
  customs inspector who seized their own souvenir. Identity twist beats statement
  paradox. #2 also shares the insurance lane with ~8 field entries.
- #1 vs #3: #1 wins narrowly — #3 ("one previous owner: you... one-star review")
  is strong and specific, but its reveal is a punchline about a review; #1's
  reveal changes who the reader is. Margin 2.
- #1 vs #4: #1 wins — #4 (Grandma, 12 times) is warm and specific, but the reveal
  ("not telling you where she went") is a shrug, not a reframe; #1's last clause
  lands harder. Margin 2.
- #1 vs #5: #1 wins — #5's loop ("future self will buy it back") is neat, but it
  is a second-order paradox the reader must unwind; #1's reveal is instant.
  Margin 2.
- #1 vs #6: #1 wins — #6 ("filed a complaint about you in 1998") is funny and
  specific, but it leans on the crowded duplicate-self lane and its reveal is a
  workplace gag, not a time-travel hazard turned into the joke (criterion 2).
  Margin 3.
- #1 vs #7: #1 wins — #7 ("claims must be filed before the machine is built") is
  a clean paradox and the tightest (90 chars), but it has no character and no
  specific number; #1 has both (1974, the inspector = you). Margin 2.
- #1 vs #8: #1 wins — #8 ("panicking is what caused the paradox") is clever but
  self-referential and wordy; it explains the joke instead of landing it.
  Margin 3.

## Champion

**CAUTION: Do not bring souvenirs from the future. Customs seized yours in 1974,
and you are the inspector.** — 105 chars / 105 bytes.

- Criterion 1: a genuine warning label for a time machine (souvenirs from the
  future can only exist via time travel).
- Criterion 2: the hazard is a real paradox — the traveller's own souvenir was
  seized in the past by the traveller themself.
- Criterion 3: the surprise is saved for the last clause ("and you are the
  inspector") — the reader is the authority, not the victim.
- Criterion 4: straight face throughout — "CAUTION:", "Customs seized yours",
  bureaucratic calm.
- Criterion 5: every phrase works; 105 chars, no filler. Specific year (1974)
  and a named role (the inspector) per the winner pattern (specific numbers /
  named characters).
- Fresh angle: no field entry touches souvenirs/customs; the crowded lanes
  (meet-yourself, grandfather, butterflies, warranty, lend-money) are all avoided
  without avoiding them blindly — the customs angle simply has no good execution
  in the field yet.
- Tie-break bytes = 105. The 90-char #7 is smaller, but bytes only break exact
  ties on judge win rate, and #1 wins decisively on the judge criteria.
